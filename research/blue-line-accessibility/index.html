Measuring access around the CTA Blue Line

A spatial analysis of how practical transit access varies across the neighborhoods surrounding a single rail line.

StatusCompleted
SettingChicago
MethodSpatial analysis
OutputProject notes

The question

How much of the city is meaningfully accessible to a Blue Line station, and how does the answer change when access is defined by real travel constraints rather than a simple radius?

Why it matters

A transit line on a map does not guarantee equal access. Street geometry, walking distance, service patterns, and the distribution of people and destinations can all change who benefits.

Data and method

The project combines station locations with surrounding spatial data and compares catchment assumptions. The purpose is to make the measurement choices visible rather than allow a polished map to imply more certainty than the inputs support.

Interpretation and limitations

Primary takeaway. Accessibility is a property of both the network and its surrounding geography; distance to a line alone is an incomplete measure.

A full public release should document data vintage, walking-network assumptions, geographic units, and sensitivity to the selected access threshold.

What I worked on

I developed the spatial-accessibility analysis and the visual framing of the results. Project artifacts and exact estimates will be linked here when prepared for public release.
